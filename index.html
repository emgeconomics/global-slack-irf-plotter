Impulse Response Functions (IRFs) Plotter

Digging deeper on shock transmission and international linkages: this interactive plotter explores how productivity and monetary-policy shocks propagate in a workhorse two-country New Keynesian model when structural parameters, shock processes, and monetary-policy rules are modified. It is designed as a companion to The Global Slack Hypothesis and the Open-Economy Phillips Curve by Enrique Martínez García and Mark A. Wynne, and to Good Policies or Good Luck? New Insights on Globalization and the International Monetary Policy Transmission Mechanism by Enrique Martínez García. The black lines report a benchmark parameterization, while the blue lines report an alternative specification chosen by the user, making it possible to trace endogenous and exogenous cross-country spillovers as well as the role of monetary policy.

Implementation Notes

This browser-based plotter accompanies The Global Slack Hypothesis and the Open-Economy Phillips Curve by Enrique Martínez García and Mark A. Wynne, and Good Policies or Good Luck? New Insights on Globalization and the International Monetary Policy Transmission Mechanism by Enrique Martínez García. The Dallas Fed link is a placeholder for the public version of the Global Slack paper. The plotter solves a workhorse two-country New Keynesian model in which Home and Foreign economies are linked through trade, relative prices, monetary policy, and cross-country shock transmission. The pricing assumption is producer-currency pricing, abbreviated PCP, meaning that exporters set prices in their own currency. In this setting, nominal exchange-rate movements pass through fully to import prices.

The foreign import-share parameter is pinned down by zero steady-state trade balance: ξ* = n(1 - ξ)/(1 - n). The interface therefore lets users choose n and ξ, but not ξ*. The controls impose the economically natural home-bias restriction ξ ≥ n.

Shock magnitudes are expressed in innovation standard deviations. In a Gaussian benchmark, one, two, and three standard deviations correspond roughly to 68, 95, and 99.7 percent bands. The impact response is plotted as quarter 1, and the default horizon reports 40 quarters. Innovation correlations are retained as displayed parameters for consistency with the stochastic model, but a deterministic one-shock impulse response is driven by the selected innovation only.

The model combines aggregate demand equations, Phillips curves for inflation, monetary-policy rules, natural-rate and potential-output relationships, and stochastic processes for productivity and monetary-policy disturbances in both economies. Home and Foreign employment are computed from the log-linear production relationships, so l = y - a and l* = y* - a*.

The policy-rate and natural-rate responses are plotted in separate panels. In the relative-price panels, solid lines report the terms of trade and dashed lines report the real exchange rate. The Wicksellian monetary-policy option means that the selected policy rule tracks its own economy's natural rate; the standard Taylor-rule option responds to inflation and slack without that natural-rate tracking term. The Home and Foreign rules can be set independently.

The plotted terms of trade, real exchange rates, and trade balances are accounting relationships computed after solving the dynamic system. In the producer-currency-pricing environment, the Home terms of trade move with the productivity-driven natural relative price and with the gap between Home and Foreign slack. The Foreign terms of trade move one-for-one in the opposite direction. The real exchange rates are tied to those terms-of-trade movements through the difference in Home-good expenditure shares across the two consumption baskets. Because the model is normalized around zero steady-state trade balance, the trade-balance series report deviations from that steady-state benchmark: a positive Home value corresponds to an equal Foreign deficit, and vice versa.
